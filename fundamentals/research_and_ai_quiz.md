## Answer Key (Teacher Copy)

1. **B.** It is not an error state. The machinery that writes fluently is the same machinery that writes falsely, so it has no signal telling it which one it just did.

2. **B.** The `site:` operator restricts results to one domain. Note there is no space after the colon.

3. **B.** This is the F in SIFT, find better coverage. Answer C is the trap: asking an AI to check a claim it might have produced is not verification.

4. **B.** Copilot. Claude requires users to be 18 with no school exception, so answer A is wrong even though the school does pay for it.
   *This question exists because the old version of this lesson pointed students at Claude first.*

5. **D.** Trace to the original. Students will follow a claim to an article and stop there. The article is a secondary source, and the summary is frequently wrong about what the study actually found.

6. **Learning:** "Explain how CSS Grid works with a simple example so I can write my own." **Cheating:** "Write my CSS Grid assignment." The test is whether they are using it to understand or to avoid understanding.
   *Full credit needs both examples and a stated reason.*

7. Good answers ask for explanation, practice, or a check on their own thinking. For example: "Give me 5 practice questions on loops, do not show answers until I try", or "I think a for loop always runs 10 times, am I right?"
   *Not acceptable: any prompt that asks for the finished product.*

8. Conclude that the claim is unsupported, and treat it the same as a made-up citation. A working link is not verification, only the content is. Next step is to search for the claim independently, and if nothing credible supports it, drop it.
   *This is the most valuable question on the sheet. A link that resolves but does not support the claim is the failure mode students are least likely to catch on their own.*
